# v4 results: blending rules and similarity on the large catalog (k=5, 1985 songs, 60 listeners in 10 archetypes)

Generated by `python -m experiments.run_v4`. There is no real listening data, so two synthetic protocols stand in for two opposite ideas of taste: **genre taste** (a listener likes whole genres, which favours label-based rules) and **audio taste** (a listener likes songs that sound like their seeds in a richer feature space the recommender cannot fully see, which favours similarity). Metric: NDCG@5. Intervals resample whole archetypes.

**Declared rule:** best worst-case score across the two protocols, ties to the smaller weight. It selects **Rules + centroid similarity (weight 8), no retrieval filter** (genre taste 0.618, audio taste 0.664).

**Shipped default:** Rules + centroid similarity (weight 3), no retrieval filter. That rule's pick gives up 0.33 NDCG under genre taste to gain 0.43 under audio taste. The shipped default is a judgement call at the knee of the curve instead: -0.03 under genre taste for +0.21 under audio taste, relative to rules only. Change `WEIGHTS["similarity"]` in `src/recommender.py` to move along the trade-off.

## How to read this

- No single method wins both protocols: the rules are near-perfect when taste is genre-defined and weak when it is sound-defined, and similarity does the opposite.
- Blending trades one for the other. The right weight depends on how real listeners behave, which this dataset cannot tell us.
- These are synthetic benchmarks. They show how the system behaves under two clearly stated assumptions, not how good the recommendations are for real people.
